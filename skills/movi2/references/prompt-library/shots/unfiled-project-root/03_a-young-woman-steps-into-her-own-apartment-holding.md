SCENE CONTEXT
A young woman steps into her own apartment holding a newborn, pushes the door shut behind her and leans back against it, shaken. She lets out one long breath. Then the child starts to cry and she walks the room back and forth, rocking him and trying to settle him. She is alone. She is seen in profile throughout.

ACTIVE REFERENCES
<<<mum>>>: 30yo woman, badly shaken but holding herself together, straight dark hair falling loose to her shoulders and stuck to her temples, oversized pale grey padded jacket worn open over an olive high-neck tunic, loose olive trousers, black rubber boots. Both arms are wrapped around <<<char_baby>>> against her chest: right forearm underneath taking the weight, left hand across the top of the swaddle. Face bare, no helmet, no mask. She never speaks and never sings — she only breathes, hushes and rocks. She is the only sharp subject in this shot. 100% matches the reference.
<<<char_baby>>>: hours-old newborn, face flushed and creased, swaddled in coarse pale linen cloth drawn up over the top of the head, one tiny hand escaping the wrap at the lower edge. Held vertically against <<<mum>>>'s chest, head supported at her collarbone. Eyes squeezed shut, face reddening and mouth opening as he begins to cry, small fists working, then slowly settling toward the end. Stays in her arms for the whole shot and is never lowered, never set down, never handed over. 100% matches the reference.
<<<loc_womanapt>>>: a bare apartment interior in an old residential block — mint-green painted walls, worn pale timber plank floor, a low striped fabric sofa against the wall beside a curtained window with a radiator beneath it, a small low table, a dark cabinet in the far corner, a bare pendant fixture hanging unlit from the ceiling. On one side, a tall panelled timber entrance door standing open onto the communal corridor beyond — corridor with white upper walls over a green dado, a green door with a red number plate, a lit fluorescent tube. On the other side, an open doorway through to a narrow tiled kitchen. 100% matches the reference.
Only two subjects exist in this shot: <<<mum>>> and <<<char_baby>>>. No soldiers, no agents, no pursuers, no other people, no machines. Nothing follows her in. The door stays closed once shut.

LOCATION MAP
The camera stands inside the apartment, roughly in the centre of the room, and pivots on the spot to follow her. It never crosses the line and never moves to a frontal position.
SCREEN-LEFT: the tall panelled entrance door and, beyond it, the lit communal corridor.
SCREEN-RIGHT: the open kitchen doorway, cool light spilling from it.
BEHIND HER PACING PATH: the sofa, the curtained window with its pale cold daylight, the radiator, the dark cabinet in the corner.
Her pacing path runs left to right across the room in front of the sofa and window and back again, parallel to the far wall and perpendicular to the camera axis, always 3 to 4 meters from the lens. She never walks toward the camera and never walks out of the room.
FOREGROUND: nothing between camera and subject for the whole shot.

FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING — PROFILE ONLY
The first visible frame already contains <<<mum>>> in profile, framed from the top of her chest to just above her head, entering through the door at screen-left, <<<char_baby>>> clamped against her chest and clipping the bottom edge of frame. No empty establishing frame, no wide shot of the room, no delayed reveal.
HER BODY IS PERPENDICULAR TO THE CAMERA AXIS FOR THE ENTIRE SHOT. She is seen in clean side profile or at most a shallow three-quarter, never square to the lens. Her nose, chin and brow read as a silhouette edge against the background. Her chest, her arms and the bundle read from the side. She never turns her front toward camera, never faces the lens, never presents both eyes to camera, and never turns her back fully to camera either — the profile is held throughout, switching only from left-facing to right-facing as she turns at each end of her walk.
At the door she stands with her back against the panel and her head turned in profile along the wall, so the camera still sees her side. From 0:12 she leaves the door and walks the room, and her direction of travel keeps her in profile automatically.
Camera holds a constant 3.5 meters from her at her eye height and maintains that distance by pivoting rather than by moving with her.

FORMAT MODE
SINGLE CONTINUOUS TAKE. 20 seconds, real time, no cuts, no transitions, no slow motion, no speed ramps.

OPTICS — TIGHT LONG LENS, ONE SUBJECT SHARP AT A TIME
29° diagonal field of view, 85mm-equivalent short telephoto portrait lens character, camera 3.5 meters from <<<mum>>>, held constant for the full 20 seconds. Wide open aperture, thin plane of focus.
Close framing achieved through lens reach, not physical proximity. Only one plane is sharp at any moment: her profile at the start and the end, the newborn's face in the middle. Everything else falls off according to the focal length and distance — the sofa, window and cabinet melted into soft shapes, the corridor doorway a dissolved cold rectangle, the kitchen doorway a soft wash. During the pacing the whole room streaks past behind her as unresolved soft colour while she stays sharp.
Focus stays on the subject and is corrected continuously by hand as she moves left and right, always arriving a fraction late, never hunting, never overshooting, never snapping. Three deliberate focus moves in the whole shot and no others: down to the child at 0:06, back to her at 0:10, and one soft settle onto the child at 0:17. No zoom, no focal drift.

CAMERA — HANDHELD, PIVOTING, NEVER LOCKED
Handheld throughout. A separate operator standing in the room with his own weight on his own feet, holding the long lens. The camera is never rigged to her, never mounted on her, never locked off, and never goes still — including in the held beats at the door.
The long lens magnifies every operator input: a continuous low-amplitude sway in all axes that never repeats, a slow vertical breathing cycle pumping underneath everything, roll wandering 1 to 2 degrees off level and corrected gently and continuously by hand. Framing is held loosely — she drifts off centre and is eased back, drops low and is lifted — always with small continuous corrections.
When she paces, the operator pivots on the spot to follow her: smooth hand-led pans left and right, always trailing her by a fraction so she leads the frame and is caught up with rather than perfectly centred. The pans are unhurried and slightly uneven, never whip pans, never snap corrections. The operator never walks around her and never repositions to a frontal angle.
The tilt down to the child and back up to her is one continuous gentle hand-led move each way, arriving a fraction late.
No dolly, no gimbal, no stabilizer, no steadicam, no post-stabilization, no mechanical float, no tracking alongside her, no arc, no orbit. No stumbles, no jolts, no stray to the walls or ceiling, no dutch angle, no punch-in, no moment where the operator loses her and has to find her again.

ACTION TIMING

0:00 to 0:02 — <<<mum>>> comes through the door at screen-left into frame in profile, still moving fast, chest heaving, hair stuck to her cheek, her visible eye wide and unfocused, looking ahead along her line of travel and not at the lens.

0:02 to 0:04 — Her left hand comes off the swaddle, reaches back and pushes the door shut. The door meets the frame and stops. She sets her shoulders and the back of her head against the panel, head still turned in profile along the wall. Her left hand returns to the bundle.

0:04 to 0:06 — THE BREATH. Her eye closes. Her chin lifts slightly, throat and jawline reading clean against the background. She draws one long breath in through her nose and lets it out slowly through her mouth, her shoulders dropping several centimetres as the tension goes out of them, her whole body settling back into the door.

0:06 to 0:10 — She looks down at the child and the camera tilts down with her, focus pulling onto <<<char_baby>>>: the newborn's flushed creased face against the linen, seen from the side. His face tightens, reddens, his mouth opens and he begins to cry — first a thin catch of breath, then a full rising wail, his escaped hand opening and closing. Her arms tighten around him immediately.

0:10 to 0:12 — The camera tilts back up to her profile. Her eye snaps open, the relief gone, replaced by alarm: she turns her head sharply along the wall toward the closed door, then back down to the child. Her mouth shapes a soundless hush against the crown of the swaddle. Her body never rotates toward camera.

0:12 to 0:16 — SHE WALKS THE ROOM. She pushes off the door and paces across the room from screen-left to screen-right in front of the sofa and window, moving across the frame in clean profile, at an urgent uneven walk, turning at the end of the room and coming back the other way — her profile flipping to face the opposite side as she turns. She bounces the child with a short rhythmic bob of her knees every few steps, her right arm rocking the bundle, her left hand patting the swaddle in a fast steady rhythm then smoothing it. She looks down at him constantly and twice toward the door. The camera pans with her, trailing slightly.

0:16 to 0:18 — She turns again mid-room and keeps walking, rocking harder now, pressing her cheek against the crown of his head, hushing into the cloth. His crying breaks into hitching sobs and starts to lose force. The camera settles softly onto the child at her chest, his face wet and blotchy, his hand slowing.

0:18 to 0:20 — Still walking, still rocking, more slowly now. She lifts her head and the frame comes back to her profile: exhausted, wet-eyed, a rough smile that does not reach her eye. The crying has dropped to broken snuffles. She is still pacing in profile when the shot ends.

She never speaks. She never looks into the lens. She never turns to face camera. She never screams, never shakes the child, never slides to the floor, never sets him down. The relief and the fear are both physical: the shoulders, the breath, the arms, the walking.

PHYSICS
Real gravity, real mass. Her back and shoulders take her weight against the door panel, which flexes slightly and holds. The door has real mass: it swings, meets the frame and stops dead — no rebound, no bounce, no reopening.
The pacing has real weight transfer: heel contact on the timber planks, hip shift, toe push-off, the floor giving slightly with a low creak, real deceleration and turn at each end of the walk rather than a smooth glide. The knee-bounce rocking travels up through her whole torso and into the bundle.
The oversized padded jacket has bulk, compressing where she leans against the door and swinging a frame behind her torso as she turns at each end of the room. Hair moves with delay and settles late.
<<<char_baby>>> has the weight of a real newborn: the bundle shifts against her chest on every step and every rock, absorbed by her arms, the linen folding and creasing under her fingers, the head always supported and never flopping. Real newborn skin texture, blotchy and soft, fine hair, real tears and wet flushed skin when he cries.
No floating bodies, no weightless bundle, no rubbery CG motion, no plastic doll skin, no game-engine look.

LIGHTING
The apartment is dim and stays dim, at a constant level for the full 20 seconds. No flicker, no strobing, no light going out, no blackout, no change in exposure between 0:00 and 0:20.
Primary source is the curtained window on the far wall behind her pacing path, throwing pale cold daylight through thin white curtains. Because she moves in profile across it, it rakes her from behind and the side: a hard bright edge along her brow, nose, chin, the front of her throat and the crown of the swaddle, with the camera-facing side of her face falling into soft unfilled shadow. Her profile silhouette carries the image.
Secondary sources: the cold fluorescent white spilling from the communal corridor through the doorway at screen-left, and the cool wash from the kitchen doorway at screen-right. As she paces she crosses both, so the rim light shifts from one side to the other but her profile edge is always defined and she is never swallowed by darkness.
Overall palette: desaturated green-cyan, mint walls, worn timber floor tones dissolved out of focus, deep unfilled blacks in the corners. Exposure is set for the rim on her face, not for the window, which stays clipped pale. No flat front light, no beauty fill, no warm key, no ceiling light on.
Kodak Vision3 500T, real grain, naturalistic low-key texture, grounded physical cinema, no HDR, no heavy grade, no CG gloss.

AUDIO
No music. No dialogue. <<<mum>>> never speaks and her lips never form words — only breath and a wordless hush. No subtitles.
Her breathing carries the first half: hard and ragged on entry; one long deliberate inhale and a slow shaking exhale at 0:04; then quick and tight again once the crying starts. Soft wordless hushing against the cloth from 0:11 onward, close to the microphone, never resolving into speech.
The door: a scuff of the panel, the dull wooden knock as it meets the frame, the latch clicking home, then the creak of her weight settling against it.
The newborn: a thin catching breath at 0:08 building into a full open-throated cry, sustained and raw through the pacing, breaking into hitching sobs around 0:17 and dropping to wet broken snuffles by 0:19. Never stops entirely before the shot ends.
Footsteps: her boots on timber planks, uneven and urgent, the floor creaking under her, turning and restarting at each end of the room.
Cloth: the padded jacket compressing and swinging, the linen swaddle shifting, her hand patting a steady rhythm on the fabric.
Room tone: the faint hum of the corridor fluorescent bleeding through the closed door, a low structural rumble, far-off indistinct noise from other floors. No voices, no gunfire, no footsteps outside the door.

POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS
Exactly one adult and one newborn in frame for all 20 seconds. Nobody else enters, nobody knocks, the door stays shut once closed. The child stays in her arms throughout and is never put down. <<<mum>>> is seen in profile for the entire take and never turns to face the camera. Framing stays tight on her head and shoulders and on the bundle — the camera never widens to show the whole room, and she is never lost from frame while pacing. The camera pivots on the spot, stays handheld and alive for the full 20 seconds, never crosses to a frontal angle and never locks off. Lighting level stays constant throughout. Sharp clarity on the subject in focus, natural skin colors, no ghosting, no image flicker.
