SCENE CONTEXT
Four tactical operatives advance down a Soviet-era corridor in a stacked formation, moving under control and exchanging hand signals. A camera operator precedes them, walking backwards well ahead of the stack. Single continuous take, 10 seconds, real time.

SUBJECT
<<<captain-agentv2>>>: adult male tactical operative, dark olive-green combat suit, segmented chest armor plate, heavy tactical vest with faded stenciled unit codes, black sealed composite helmet with opaque black visor — face never visible, the visor a flat black void catching the ceiling lights as a sliding highlight. Lower jaw a mechanical mask of overlapping vent plates with central audio receptor, thin cable bundle hanging from the helmet base. Black tactical gloves. Automatic rifle held in both hands. He is POINT MAN, front of the stack, nearest the lens. 100% matches the reference.
THREE ADDITIONAL OPERATIVES: identical gear, identical helmets and opaque visors, identical rifles — visually indistinguishable from <<<captain-agentv2>>> except by position and build. #2 close behind his right shoulder. #3 behind his left shoulder. #4 rear, hindmost, turning to check behind the stack. All four fully clothed and geared identically, no unit patches, no flags, no national insignia, no legible text on any gear.

LOCATION
<<<terrazzoest>>>: Soviet-era institutional interior — rough plaster walls, institutional green lower half, faded white above, worn linoleum or concrete floor, exposed pipe runs, cold fluorescent tubes at intervals along the ceiling, frosted glass panels and closed doors set into the walls at intervals. Extend the reference into a long straight corridor: the tubes receding in perspective behind the stack, doorways passing on both sides, the far end unresolved in shadow.

OPTICS — NARROW LENS
85mm equivalent, held constant for the full 10 seconds. Narrow field of view, strong telephoto compression: the four operatives stack into a dense overlapping column with very little apparent depth between them — #2, #3 and #4 crowd up behind the point man rather than reading as separate planes. The corridor walls fall almost entirely outside the frame; only a narrow strip of wall and the passing doorways are visible at the edges. The ceiling tubes compress into a tight receding chain. Shallow depth of field: the point man's helmet and chest plate sharp, #2 slightly soft, #3 and #4 progressively softer and heavier in bokeh. No zoom. No focal drift.

FRAMING
Frontal, chest-to-helmet on <<<captain-agentv2>>>, the stack advancing into the lens. Camera at his eye height or slightly below. The narrow lens forces the operator to stay well ahead of the stack — a longer working distance down the corridor. The rifle points up-forward past the camera's left shoulder, never into the lens. His visor faces camera throughout: opaque, unreadable, catching each passing tube as a hard highlight that slides across the black.

FORMATION AND BLOCKING
Tight stack, staggered — not a flat line. <<<captain-agentv2>>> leads, weapon up and sweeping ahead past the camera's left shoulder. #2 tucked to his right rear, muzzle covering the right wall and doorways. #3 to his left rear, muzzle covering the left. #4 walks last, rotating his torso to cover behind, briefly presenting his back to the lens. Spacing stays tight, roughly an arm's length, and compresses further as they slow — under 85mm compression the stack reads as a single dense mass.

ACTION
The stack advances at a controlled tactical pace — no running, no charging. Heel-to-toe placement, knees soft, weapons up and steady, torsos rotating to cover angles as they pass each doorway. The whole body moves as the weapon moves.

Signals, unspoken:
0:01 — <<<captain-agentv2>>> raises a flat gloved hand at shoulder height: the stack slows in a single beat, spacing compressing.
0:03 — He turns his helmet back over his right shoulder toward #2. #2's visor comes up to meet it. A held half-second of contact, then a two-finger point toward a doorway passing on the right. #2 breaks off his cover arc and tracks the door as they pass it, muzzle following, then rejoins.
0:05 — #3 raises a closed fist. The stack halts for under a second, all four freezing mid-stride. #3's helmet swings to the left wall, holds, then his hand opens flat and drops. They resume.
0:07 — #4 taps #3's shoulder plate twice with the back of his glove; #3 relays it forward with a touch to <<<captain-agentv2>>>'s vest. The signal travels up the stack in under a second, each man's helmet turning as it passes him.
0:09 — <<<captain-agentv2>>> gives a forward chopping motion with his left hand. The stack picks up pace slightly and keeps advancing as the shot ends.

Every signal is small, economical, professional — no theatrical gestures, no wide arm movements. The communication is entirely physical: helmet turns, glove positions, shoulder taps. No speech.

CAMERA — HANDHELD, OPERATOR WALKING BACKWARDS AHEAD OF THE STACK
85mm. The camera is a separate body walking backwards down the corridor — blind to what is behind it, feeling for the floor with the heel. The narrow lens magnifies every one of his movements: small physical inputs become large frame movements.

The operator's own physicality drives the frame:
— Backward walking gait, uneven and cautious, out of sync with the stack's cadence. Continuous 4–6mm sway, magnified by the long lens into visible frame drift.
— His breathing pumps a slow vertical cycle underneath everything, unmistakable at this focal length.
— Roll wanders 1.5–3° off level, hand-corrected in swings that overshoot. Kept restrained — under 85mm compression, more roll than this destroys legibility.
— Distance to the stack breathes constantly, and at this focal length small changes read large: he backs off and the stack shrinks toward mid-frame, then he checks and they crowd the frame.
— The framing is loose: the point man drifts low, is lifted back, sits off-centre. Re-found by hand each time, never held in a fixed position.
— The lens strays to catch a passing doorway at the frame edge, then swings back to the stack, arriving late — at 85mm the stray takes them almost entirely out of frame for a beat before recovery.
— At the fist-halt around 0:05 the operator keeps backing up out of momentum and has to check himself, opening the gap for a beat.
— Near 0:07 a heel finds the floor badly — a sharp unplanned dip and a fast recovery, the frame dropping and swinging before it settles.
— At 0:09 they accelerate and he gives ground, the compression making them appear to surge forward faster than they actually move.

Two hand-pulled focus corrections that hunt past the mark before landing — the shallow depth of field at 85mm makes both misses obvious, one leaving the point man's helmet soft for nearly a second.

NEGATIVE: no gimbal, no stabilizer, no steadicam, no tripod, no dolly, no post-stabilization, no smooth glide, no mechanical float. The camera must never feel rigidly attached to the characters. Never still — not for a single frame, including during the halt.

LIGHTING
Cold green-white fluorescent from the ceiling tubes, each one passing overhead and raking down across the stack in sequence — helmets and shoulder plates flaring as they pass under a tube, dropping into shadow between them. Under compression the tubes strobe across the stack faster and more rhythmically. Hard highlight sliding across the black visors. The corridor behind them compressed into a tight receding chain of light and shadow, the far end unresolved. Flat grey spill from frosted door panels. No warm light.
Kodak Vision3 500T pushed one stop — fine grain, cold tones, crushed blacks. 180° shutter motion blur at 24fps.

AUDIO
No music, no dialogue, no radio chatter. Hard institutional reverb — long flat corridor acoustic.
Boot placements: four sets, deliberate and controlled, overlapping but not synchronised — heel-to-toe on hard floor, quieter than a normal walk. They stop dead at the fist-halt and the corridor rings empty for a beat.
Gear: vest creak, sling shift, a plate knock as a body rotates to cover an angle. A soft double tap of a glove on a shoulder plate at 0:07.
Processed helmet breath from the point man, steady and controlled. The others fainter behind him.
Distant structural building ambient underneath — a low hum, a pipe tick.
No camera operator footsteps, no handling noise, no breath from behind the lens.

CONSTRAINTS
16:9. Single take, 10 seconds, no cuts. No slow-motion. Walking pace throughout — controlled tactical advance, never running.
85mm constant. No zoom, no focal drift, no compression change.
All four faces never visible, all visors opaque and unreadable — no eyes, no features, no reflection revealing a face.
Camera stays ahead of the stack, backing down the corridor — never circles behind, never crosses to their side.
No rifle barrel ever points into the lens at any moment — muzzles sweep the walls and doorways, past the camera, never at it.
No shots fired, no muzzle flashes, no violence, no contact with anyone. No other characters in frame — the corridor is empty ahead and behind. No blood, no bodies, no injury.
No flags, national symbols, badges, unit patches, logos or legible text on gear, walls or doors.
All four operatives must remain readable in frame for the full 10 seconds despite the compression and the shallow depth of field — the layering of the stack is the shot. #4 must stay visible at the back, never fully lost to bokeh.
No text or subtitles. Photorealistic, no CG gloss.
