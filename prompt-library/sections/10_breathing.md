## scene-10-07 · Close-up on an armored officer's face, seated.

`BREATHING LOCK — THREE STAGES, NONE OF THEM RELIEF` · Close-up · Handheld · 15s · **reused verbatim in 2 shots**

Used in: [scene-10-07](../shots/scene-10/07_close-up-on-an-armored-officer-s-face-seated.md), [unfiled-project-root-08](../shots/unfiled-project-root/08_close-up-on-an-armored-officer-s-face-seated.md)

````text
He is already breathing badly at the first frame, before he touches anything. This is not caused by the gear and it must not read as relief at any point.
1. From 0:00, inside the helmet: fast, shallow, effortful breathing through the pickup — compressed, band-limited, hollow, close. Uneven, catching twice, never settling.
2. From the helmet coming off: the compression drops away and the breath enters the room, but stays muffled through the thick gaiter fabric over his mouth. Closer and rawer, still damped.
3. From the gaiter coming down: fully open. Wet, close, unfiltered, much louder, every intake and release audible. This is the biggest sound change in the shot.
The breathing itself never changes across all three stages. Only what we can hear of it does. It does not slow, does not deepen, does not resolve into calm. After the gaiter comes down it is, if anything, worse, because nothing is holding it in.
Visibly and continuously once the face is uncovered: the nostrils flaring on every inhale, the throat working, the shoulders lifting at the bottom edge of frame, a faint pulse visible at the side of the neck.
This is not crying and must never become crying. No sob, no gasp for effect, no shuddering catch, no whimper, no tears, no wetness in the eyes beyond the strain of breathing, no vocalisation of any kind. It is a body that has been working too hard for too long inside a sealed shell.
````
