REGISTER — SENIOR ACADEMIC BEING INTERVIEWED. He is a man who has taught this material for thirty years and has been interviewed about it many times. Assured, unhurried, completely at ease with both the subject and the situation. He does not search for words and does not hesitate.

He is not addressing an audience, not opening a lecture, not making a statement for the record. He is talking to one person standing a metre from the lens, and the size of the delivery matches that distance — conversational volume, conversational energy, no projection.
Interview-specific speech behaviour, all of it small:
— He speaks slightly ACROSS the camera rather than to it, the sound aimed a few degrees off-axis toward the interviewer.
— Sentence stress falls where a person places it in conversation, not where a reader places it. Some words get swallowed slightly under the ones around them; the phrase carries the meaning, not each individual word.
— Inside the run, clauses connect the way speech connects and not the way text does: the last syllable of one sentence carries straight into the first of the next, with the pitch continuing rather than resetting.
— One phrase inside the run is delivered a fraction quieter than the rest, dropping in level mid-thought the way people do when the point is obvious to them.
THE SHAPE OF THE TAKE IS TWO BLOCKS AND ONE GAP. Everything is compressed into the run, then nothing for three and a half seconds, then one slow closing line. The contrast between the density of the run and the emptiness of the pause is the entire rhythmic design, and the short overall duration makes that contrast sharper rather than softer.
— The run goes fast and never stops. He is clearing ground he considers already cleared, and he has cleared it in interviews before. He does not pause for effect anywhere inside it because none of it is the point.
— The pause is long relative to everything around it. He does not fill it, does not hum, does not shift, does not look away. A man who has taught for thirty years is completely comfortable with a silence he has chosen, and comfortable holding another person's eyes through it.
— The final line is the slowest thing in the take. He is not selling it. He is finishing an answer.
Breath pattern: one breath before the run, none inside it; one deep, audible, unhurried breath during the pause; a normal breath before the final line. Breath never falls between the sentences of the run.
Micro-variation within the delivery: no two sentences share the same volume or the same terminal pitch. Occasional very slight lengthening of a vowel where a non-native speaker naturally takes a fraction longer to place a word — never enough to read as hesitation, only enough to sound like a real person rather than a read.
NOTHING IS RUSHED TO FIT THE DURATION. The shorter runtime comes from less dead time, not from faster speech. The run keeps its brisk conversational pace and the closing line keeps its slowness; neither is compressed, clipped or sped up.
"Testing. Testing." and "Can you see the fish?" sit in a different register again — ordinary, flat, practical, faster than everything after the cut, and addressed to the crew rather than to the interviewer. The interview register begins only after the cut, and the change is audible.
He is patient rather than persuasive, explaining a settled matter to someone who has not understood it yet. Faintly weary of having to explain it again. Never enthusiastic, never salesmanlike, never conspiratorial, never confiding.
Lip movement matches the phonemes, phrasing and length of delivery exactly, with no exaggeration.
No subtitles, no captions, no on-screen text.
