An armored officer opens a closed apartment door from the corridor and comes slowly inside. He leaves the door open behind him. He moves through the empty room, his helmet stopping on one direction at a time, inspecting the space. He is alone. He is seen in profile throughout.

SINGLE CONTINUOUS TAKE. 15 seconds, real time, no cuts, no transitions, no slow motion, no speed ramps. Wordless. No dialogue, no subtitles, no captions.

NO MUSIC — ABSOLUTE

No score, no soundtrack, no theme, no cue, no sting, no swell, no underscore, no ambient music bed.

The only sounds are those listed in AUDIO. Silence is correct. Music is not.

<<<loc_womanapt>>>: a bare apartment interior in an old residential block — mint-green painted walls, worn pale timber plank floor, a low striped fabric sofa against the wall beside a curtained window with a radiator beneath it, a small low table, a dark cabinet in the far corner, a bare pendant fixture hanging unlit from the ceiling. On one side, a tall panelled timber entrance door onto the communal corridor beyond — corridor with white upper walls over a green dado, a green door with a red number plate, a lit fluorescent tube. On the other side, an open doorway through to a narrow tiled kitchen. 100% matches the reference.
Only one subject exists in this shot: <<<captain-agentv4>>>. No other agents, no civilians, no children, no machines, nobody in the corridor, nobody in the kitchen. Nobody follows him in. The room is empty and stays empty.

DOOR LOCK — CLOSED, THEN OPENED, THEN LEFT OPEN
At the first frame the entrance door at screen-left is CLOSED. It is a tall panelled timber door with a handle, seen from inside the apartment.
He opens it from the OUTSIDE, from the corridor. The handle turns first, then the door swings inward into the room, slowly and steadily, pushed by a gloved hand from beyond it.
Once open, the door STAYS OPEN for the rest of the take. He does not close it, does not push it back, does not touch it again. The corridor beyond stays visible and lit at screen-left for the whole shot.
The open doorway is a permanent element of the frame edge from 0:03 onward: a dissolved cold rectangle of corridor light behind and to the left of him, and the way he came in, left standing open.
No second door, no other doorway, no invented opening.

LOCATION MAP
The camera stands inside the apartment, roughly in the centre of the room, and pivots on the spot to follow him. It never crosses the line and never moves to a frontal position.
SCREEN-LEFT: the tall panelled entrance door and, beyond it, the lit communal corridor.
SCREEN-RIGHT: the open kitchen doorway, cool light spilling from it.
BEHIND HIS PATH: the sofa, the curtained window with its pale cold daylight, the radiator, the dark cabinet in the corner.
His path runs left to right across the room in front of the sofa and window, parallel to the far wall and perpendicular to the camera axis, always 3 to 4 metres from the lens. He never walks toward the camera and never walks out of the room.
FOREGROUND: nothing between camera and subject for the whole shot.

FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING — PROFILE ONLY
The first visible frame holds the closed entrance door at screen-left, framed tight, with a sliver of the room's mint-green wall beside it. Nobody is in frame yet. The handle is at the centre of interest. This is the only moment in the shot without him in it, and it lasts under a second.
From 0:02 he is in frame and HIS BODY IS PERPENDICULAR TO THE CAMERA AXIS FOR THE REST OF THE SHOT. He is seen in clean side profile or at most a shallow three-quarter, never square to the lens. The curve of the dome helmet, the side hardware, the near lenses of the optical cluster seen edge-on, and the line of the gaiter read as a silhouette edge against the background. His plate carrier, his arms and the weapon read from the side. He never turns his front toward camera, never faces the lens, never presents the full optical cluster to camera, and never turns his back fully to camera either — the profile is held throughout, switching only from left-facing to right-facing as he turns at the end of his crossing.
Camera holds a constant 3.5 metres from him at helmet height and maintains that distance by pivoting rather than by moving with him.

HOW HE INSPECTS — NO FACE, NO EYELINE
He has no visible eyes and no readable expression. Nothing about his attention can be shown through a face, and none must be invented for him.
Inspection is expressed ONLY through the orientation of the helmet and the alignment of the body behind it:
- The helmet stops. It holds on one direction for a beat, long enough to read as attention, then moves to the next. It does not sweep continuously and it does not scan smoothly like a machine.
- Each move is a real lateral rotation of the neck carrying a heavy helmet: it starts, decelerates, and settles with a fractional overshoot and correction.
- The torso follows the helmet a beat late on the larger turns, the plate carrier and the weapon coming round after the head has already arrived.
- He never returns twice to the same direction. The sequence of directions is methodical and does not double back. This is what makes it read as procedure rather than as nervousness.
- His shoulders stay level and his stance stays balanced. He does not flinch, does not startle, does not react to anything.
No head tilts to suggest curiosity, no lowering to suggest doubt, no quick jerks to suggest fear. The attention is flat and administrative.
Because the camera is locked to his profile, we never see what he is looking at. The room he is inspecting stays dissolved behind him for the entire take.

FACE LOCK — NO FACE
<<<captain-agentv4>>>'s face is fully occluded and stays that way. The four-lens optical cluster hangs over the upper and mid face. A thick padded neck gaiter covers the jaw, mouth and throat. There is no visible mouth, no visible eyes, no visible skin.
He never removes his helmet, never lifts anything, never turns toward the lens. Nothing behind the lenses ever becomes visible: no eyes, no face, no silhouette, no reflected face.
No lip movement, no jaw animation, no expression is rendered on the helmet or the gaiter. The gaiter is thick structured fabric, not skin: it does not deform into lip shapes and does not ripple.

LIGHT EMISSION LOCK
The four optical cluster lenses stay dark, dead, unlit glass for the entire take.
No blue glow, no cyan glow, no white glow, no glow of any colour from any lens. No internal illumination behind the lenses, no emissive rings, no lit apertures, no scanner beams, no projected light, no laser lines, no sweeping scan.
No LEDs anywhere: not on the helmet, not on the side hardware, not on the plate carrier, not on the ID placard, not on the weapon, not on optics or sights. No status lights, no indicator lights, no recorder light, no blinking dots. No weapon-mounted lamp switched on.
No light spills from the helmet onto the gaiter, the shoulder, the walls or the floor. No colored bounce from equipment onto any surface.
The lenses may only carry faint passive reflections of the window and the corridor light. A reflection is never a source.

OPTICS — TIGHT LONG LENS, ONE SUBJECT SHARP AT A TIME
29° diagonal field of view, 85mm-equivalent short telephoto portrait lens character, camera 3.5 metres from <<<captain-agentv4>>>, held constant for the full 15 seconds. Wide open aperture, thin plane of focus.
Close framing achieved through lens reach, not physical proximity. Only one plane is sharp at any moment: the door handle at the very start, his helmet through most of the shot, his gloved hands and the weapon in the middle. Everything else falls off according to the focal length and distance — the sofa, window and cabinet melted into soft shapes, the open corridor doorway a dissolved cold rectangle, the kitchen doorway a soft wash. During the crossing the whole room streaks past behind him as unresolved soft colour while he stays sharp.
Focus stays on the subject and is corrected continuously by hand as he moves left and right, always arriving a fraction late, never hunting, never overshooting, never snapping. Three deliberate focus moves in the whole shot and no others: onto his helmet as he clears the doorway at 0:03, down to his gloved hands on the weapon at 0:06, and back up to the near lenses of the optical cluster at 0:12. No zoom, no focal drift.
Straight lines stay straight. No barrel distortion, no fisheye curve, no wide-angle expansion, no stretched proportions on the helmet.

CAMERA — HANDHELD, PIVOTING, NEVER LOCKED
Handheld throughout. A separate operator standing in the room with his own weight on his own feet, holding the long lens. The camera is never rigged to him, never mounted on him, never locked off, and never goes still — including in the held beat after he enters.
The long lens magnifies every operator input: a continuous low-amplitude sway in all axes that never repeats, a slow vertical breathing cycle pumping underneath everything, roll wandering 1 to 2 degrees off level and corrected gently and continuously by hand. Framing is held loosely — he drifts off centre and is eased back, drops low and is lifted — always with small continuous corrections.

The pans are unhurried and slightly uneven, never whip pans, never snap corrections. The operator never walks around him and never repositions to a frontal angle.
The camera does NOT follow his helmet turns. When the head rotates, the frame stays where it is and lets the helmet move within it. The camera never pans off to reveal what he is looking at.
The tilt down to his hands and back up to the helmet is one continuous gentle hand-led move each way, arriving a fraction late.
