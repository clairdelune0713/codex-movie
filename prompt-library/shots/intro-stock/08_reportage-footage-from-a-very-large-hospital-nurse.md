SCENE CONTEXT
Reportage footage from a very large hospital nursery. Cots across a wide hall, newborns in them, several nurses working. Single shot, five seconds. No dialogue.

OUTPUT SETTINGS
SINGLE SHOT. 5 seconds, real time, no cuts, no transitions, no slow motion, no speed ramps.
Wordless. No dialogue, no subtitles, no captions, no lower thirds, no watermarks, no timecode burn-in, no logos.

REPORTAGE TREATMENT — THIS IS THE GOVERNING STYLE
News or documentary reportage: a single camera operator inside a real hospital, recording because it is happening. Not stock, not cinema, not staged, not a promotional health film.
LIGHTING: available light only, and clearly so. Overhead fluorescent, uneven, slightly green, with poor falloff and some areas underlit. Nothing added, nothing shaped, no fill, no bounce, no key, no warm glow over the cots.
COLOUR: uncorrected feeling. Slightly green from the fluorescents, mildly noisy in the shadows, not graded for beauty and specifically not warmed.
EXPOSURE: broadly correct but not perfect. Slight over on the white bedding, slight under toward the edges. One small auto-exposure adjustment as the framing shifts.
PRESENCE: the operator does not have privileged access. The nurses work past the camera without accommodating it. Part of the view is obstructed.
FORBIDDEN: tripod stillness, clean symmetrical framing, designed camera moves, shaped lighting, cinematic contrast, film emulation, slow motion, soft warm nursery lighting, anything tender in the treatment, anything that suggests the scene was arranged for the camera.

CAMERA — HANDHELD BUT LEVEL
Handheld, shoulder-carried, by an experienced operator. The camera is NOT tilted: the horizon sits level and stays level for all five seconds. No dutch angle, no canted frame, no crooked horizon.
The reportage quality is in the movement, not the tilt: a slow lateral drift corrected late at around 0:03, a small vertical float from the operator's stance, and a brief focus hunt at 0:01 before settling on the middle ground.
Framing is functional rather than composed — not centred, headroom approximate — but upright and readable.
No dolly, no gimbal, no slider, no crane, no stabilised glide, no designed move, no pan, no push, no zoom.

NO DUPLICATION — ABSOLUTE, AND THE METHOD FOR ACHIEVING IT
A hundred cots is a grid, and a grid is a tiling pattern. This is the highest-risk image in the whole project and the coverage is built specifically to defeat it:
THE COTS ARE NOT IN A GRID. They are in IRREGULAR CLUSTERS: loose blocks of six to twelve cots, separated by aisles of DIFFERENT widths, with the rows in one cluster NOT aligned with the rows in the next. Some clusters sit at a slight angle to the others. Some cots are pulled out of line, one turned sideways, two pushed together, one standing alone in an aisle.
NO ROW RUNS AWAY FROM THE CAMERA toward a vanishing point. The clusters are seen from the side, across the frame. There is no central aisle receding, no perspective funnel, no diminishing repetition into distance.
THE HALL READS LATERALLY, NOT IN DEPTH. It extends past both frame edges — the cots continue off-screen left and off-screen right — and a wall closes the depth about twelve metres back and is visible. That is how the scale is communicated: sideways, never forward.
NO FACE IS EVER RESOLVED. At this distance the newborns are small pale shapes in the cots, legible as babies but never as faces. The nurses are seen from behind, from the side, cropped by the frame edge or bent over so their faces are away from the lens. Not one face, adult or infant, is legible anywhere in the shot. If no face is legible, no face can be repeated.
EVERY COT IS VISIBLY DIFFERENT in its bedding: different blanket colours and patterns, different amounts of cover, one with a folded cloth over the rail, several with side rails down and others up, a chart clipped to some and not others. Same trolley type, visibly different use.
NO ARCHITECTURAL REPETITION: no corridor, no vanishing point, no rank of identical light fittings receding, no grid of identical windows, no row of identical doors, no repeating columns. The ceiling fittings are irregularly placed and several are off.
NO EXTERIOR: no window, no view out, no city, no buildings, no daylight.
Forbidden throughout: tiling, fractal repetition, mirrored halves, symmetrical duplication, evenly spaced repeating objects, repeated bodies at diminishing scale, any two nurses or two cots that look the same.

THE HALL
A very large nursery hall inside a hospital, a converted ward or assembly space. High ceiling. Pale institutional walls, scuffed at skirting height. Worn vinyl floor.
Roughly ONE HUNDRED cot trolleys visible across the hall — clear moulded plastic bassinets on wheeled metal frames — arranged in the irregular clusters described above, with more implied beyond both frame edges.
Overhead fluorescent fittings, irregularly placed, some off, so parts of the hall are noticeably darker than others.
A steel supply trolley at one side with folded cloths and a small tray. A wall-mounted sanitiser dispenser.
Nothing else: no posters, no charts on the walls, no toys, no decoration, no signage, no branding, no readable text anywhere.
A real working room, unremarkable, lit by whatever is in the ceiling.

THE PEOPLE
FOUR NURSES visible, working in different parts of the hall, each clearly a different person: one tall in pale blue scrubs with hair tied back, one shorter in green scrubs with a cardigan over them, one heavier with short grey hair, one younger with a long dark plait. All in nitrile gloves.
They are doing different things at different distances: one bent low over a cot in the near-middle ground, one walking between clusters carrying folded cloths, one standing writing on a clipboard at the far side, one half out of frame at the edge with her back to the lens.
NO NURSE'S FACE IS RESOLVED. They are seen from behind, from the side, bent away, or cropped.
THE NEWBORNS: roughly a hundred, small pale swaddled shapes in the cots. Some visible as a head and a bit of blanket, some just as a shape under a cover, several barely visible at all from this angle. None is a subject and none is legible as a face.
Nobody else. No doctors, no parents, no visitors, no security, nobody in a doorway, nobody looking at the camera.

WHAT IS HAPPENING — A ROUTINE SHIFT
The nurses are working a normal round in a very large ward. Nothing is dramatised. Nobody is distressed. No baby is lifted out, removed, taken away or handled roughly. No parent appears or is implied.
The mood is administrative: neither sinister nor tender. The scale is the only remarkable thing, and the coverage does not comment on it.

THE SHOT (0:00 to 0:05)
Handheld wide, camera at chest height, standing in one of the aisles between two clusters of cots, looking ACROSS the hall rather than down it.
The frame is wide across: irregular clusters of cots fill the width and continue past both edges. A wall closes the depth about twelve metres back and is visible. Smoke-free, clean, ordinary.
In the near foreground at one side, out of focus and cutting into the frame: the end rail of a cot and part of its bedding, close to the lens. It stays there.
Across the hall: the four nurses, each at a different distance and doing something different. Nothing is synchronised.
Across the five seconds: the nurse in the near-middle ground straightens up from a cot and moves one step to the next one, seen from behind. The nurse carrying cloths crosses part of the frame at the far side and is briefly occluded by a cluster. The other two barely move.
Ambient movement only: no event, no incident, nothing to look at. It is a room working.
Focus hunts at 0:01 and settles on the middle ground. The camera drifts laterally and corrects late at 0:03. Exposure adjusts slightly once on the white bedding.
Level frame throughout, upright, staying upright.
END.

PERFORMANCE LOCK — NOBODY ACTS
No one performs an emotion. This is being recorded, not staged.
The nurses are neither cold nor warm. They are working a shift and they are slightly tired. Nobody lingers over a cot, nobody smiles at a baby, nobody looks sad, nobody looks at the camera.
The newborns behave like newborns: still or nearly, occasional small involuntary movement, nothing coordinated. They are not animated with adult expression, do not sit up, do not look at the camera. They are not shown in distress.
No two nurses move in sync. No two babies move in sync. No wave of unified movement passes through the room.
Surfaces are real and unremarkable: worn mattresses, creased sheets, scuffed frames, marked floor.

PHYSICS
Real gravity, real mass, real material behaviour.
The nurses move with the weight of people who have been standing for hours: unhurried, with real footfalls on vinyl.
Cot mattresses compress and hold a dent. Swaddle cloth is coarse, creases and holds folds.
The wheeled frames are stationary; nothing rolls, nothing drifts.
Nitrile gloves crease at the knuckles.
No floating motion, no rubbery CG motion, no game-engine look, no crowd or ward moving as a single mesh.

LIGHTING
Available light only: overhead fluorescent, uneven, slightly green, with poor falloff. Several fittings off, so parts of the hall are darker than others and the unevenness is visible across the frame.
The fittings are IRREGULARLY placed: not a repeating grid, not evenly spaced, not symmetrical.
White bedding bounces some light up, which is why the cots read as pale shapes across the room. That comes from the sheets, not from a lighting decision.
Nothing shaped, nothing added. Specifically: no warm glow over the cots, no soft key, no halo, no golden tone, no tenderness in the light.
The light does not change across the shot and does not flicker.
No window, no daylight visible.
No red light anywhere: no scanner, no beam, no laser, no indicator, no LED.
No theatrical front light, no beauty fill, no studio key, no rim light, no light from camera position, no golden grade, no coloured light, no vignette, no cinematic contrast.

AUDIO
Diegetic only, and this is the complete list. Nothing else is added.
1. Room tone: a very large hard-surfaced interior, reverberant, with a ventilation hum and a broad fluorescent ballast buzz. Unchanging across the five seconds.
2. From the cots: a thin sparse layer of newborn sounds spread across the room — a snuffle somewhere, one brief unsettled murmur that stops, small and distant. NOT a hall full of crying. The room is surprisingly quiet, and that is correct.
3. The nurses: soft shoes on vinyl, one cot frame creaking as someone leans on it, the rustle of cloth.
4. A distant muffled hospital background beyond the hall, unidentifiable, very low.
5. Faint camera handling noise: the operator shifting, fabric against the body.
Nothing else. Nobody speaks. No dialogue, no words, no cooing, no shushing, no announcement, no tannoy, no synthetic voice, no radio, no beeps, no monitors, no alarms.
No reporter voice, no narration, no piece to camera.
No screaming, no wailing, no distress.
NO MUSIC of any kind, at any point, at any volume: no score, no library music, no lullaby, no drone, no pad, no sustained tone, no rhythmic element. Silence is correct. Music is not.
