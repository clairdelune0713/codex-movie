SCENE CONTEXT
A cold institutional stairwell seen from high above, looking down the open shaft. Four tactical
operators climb up through the frame, flight by flight, in a staggered file, growing larger as they
rise toward the camera. The camera does not move. 5 seconds.

ACTIVE REFERENCES
<<<video_1>>>: reference for ACTION AND SPATIAL BLOCKING ONLY. Take from it: the number of operators,
their formation and staggered spacing, their route around the stairwell, their pace, their body
carriage and the way they read from a high angle. DO NOT take the location from <<<video_1>>> — its lower
stairs are geometrically wrong and must not be reproduced. The location comes entirely from
<<<loc_stairs2>>>.
<<<loc_stairs2>>>: institutional stairwell, square spiral, seen from high above looking down the open
shaft. Pale plaster walls, cold and stained. A long fluorescent tube mounted vertically on the wall
at screen-LEFT with a small dark fitting above and below it. Concrete stairs with dark anti-slip
nosings on every tread. Metal railings with close vertical balusters running around every landing
and flight, the top rail worn back to bare brown metal in places. Another fluorescent tube mounted
on the underside of each landing, glowing pale green-white, visible receding down the shaft. Every
level repeats the same rectangular geometry, diminishing in perspective toward a dark opening at the
bottom of the well. Teal-green ambient, deep shadow at the frame edges. The stairwell descends
continuously and correctly through every visible level — no false floor, no flat dark rectangle
closing off the bottom prematurely, no impossible or broken flight anywhere.
<<<team2>>>: four tactical operators. Olive-grey full kit — field jacket, plate carrier with MOLLE
webbing and front pouches, grey dome helmet with a front-mounted multi-lens night vision array,
gloves, rifle. No faces visible from this angle. 100% matches the reference.

LOCATION MAP
Camera is fixed high above the stairwell, looking down into the open shaft at a steep angle,
slightly canted, exactly matching the framing of <<<loc_stairs2>>>.
Screen-LEFT: the pale wall with its vertical fluorescent tube, and the LEFT-HAND FLIGHT running
beside it.
Screen-CENTRE: the landing beneath the lit tube — the crossing point.
Screen-RIGHT: the RIGHT-HAND FLIGHT running along the right-hand wall, climbing toward the top of
frame and toward the camera level.
Screen-CENTRE-RIGHT: the open shaft with its concentric rectangles of railing receding into depth.
THE ROUTE — <<<team2>>> climb UPWARD. Each man comes up the LEFT-HAND FLIGHT, reaches the landing under
the lit tube, crosses it from LEFT to RIGHT, turns ninety degrees at the right-hand corner, and
continues UP the RIGHT-HAND FLIGHT toward the top of frame.
They are moving toward the camera, not away from it. They get LARGER in frame as the shot runs.
Only these four people exist in the stairwell.

FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING
The first frame matches <<<loc_stairs2>>> exactly in framing, camera position, camera angle, cant,
architecture, lighting and colour.
<<<team2>>> are already in the frame and already moving — no empty establishing frame.
Their positions at the first frame: the file is spread across more than one level, deeper in the
shaft.
The LEAD operator is highest and largest — already on the landing under the lit tube, near its left
corner, starting to cross.
Behind him a large gap, then the SECOND operator lower down, part way up the left-hand flight,
noticeably smaller.
The THIRD and FOURTH are tighter to each other, further down still, on the level below, small in
frame and partly in shadow.
The spacing is uneven — one large gap after the lead, then two tighter ones.
All four are seen from above: helmets, shoulders, the tops of plate carriers and the rear webbing,
boots on the treads. No faces are visible at any point.
Exactly four people. No fifth operator, no duplicates, no bystanders.

FORMAT MODE
Single continuous uncut take. 5 seconds. No cuts. Real-time motion, no slow motion.
Camera completely locked — mounted, static, no handheld movement of any kind.

OPTICS
Match <<<loc_stairs2>>> exactly: mild wide lens character, deep focus. Straight architectural lines stay
rectilinear — no fisheye curve, no barrel bow on the railings or the wall.
Everything is readable from the nearest railing to the bottom of the shaft, with only the deepest
levels falling slightly soft.
Focus is locked and never moves. No zoom, no rack focus, no lens drift, no focus following anyone.

CAMERA
Fixed high-angle mount, static for the entire 5 seconds.
No pan, no tilt, no push, no crane, no drift, no handheld wobble, no reframe, no rotation.
The camera does NOT follow any operator. They climb up out of the frame; the camera stays exactly
where it is. Frame one and the final frame are identical in composition.

ACTION TIMING
0:00–5:00 — All four climb continuously for the whole duration. Nobody stops, nobody pauses, nobody
turns around, nobody descends.
They move at a controlled working pace — deliberate and steady, not running, not sprinting, not
creeping. The pace never changes.
Each operator climbs a flight, reaches a landing, turns ninety degrees around the corner of the
shaft, and continues up the next flight. From this angle each turn reads as the body rotating and
the helmet swinging to a new heading.
0:00–1:60 — The lead crosses the landing left to right and turns at the right corner onto the
right-hand flight. The second reaches the landing behind him. The rear two continue up from below.
1:60–3:40 — The lead climbs the right-hand flight, growing steadily in frame as he comes toward the
camera. The second crosses the landing and turns. The third arrives at the landing. The fourth is
still on the flight below.
3:40–5:00 — The lead reaches the top of the right-hand flight and exits past the upper edge of
frame. The second is well up the right-hand flight and large in frame. The third has turned the
corner. The fourth is crossing the landing.
At the final frame the depths of the shaft below them are empty — they have all come up out of it.
The staggered spacing is maintained throughout. They never bunch into a compact block and never
spread into an evenly spaced line.
As each man passes under a landing tube he brightens hard for a moment, then goes dim in the stretch
between, then brightens again at the next one. Each is on his own cycle, so the four brighten and
dim independently.
No dialogue. Nobody speaks. Nobody looks up toward camera. No face is visible at any point.

PHYSICS
Real body weight climbing stairs — the effort direction is UP and it must be visible.
Each step is a lift, not a drop: the leading foot places on the next tread, the knee and hip extend
to drive the body upward, the trailing leg follows, and the torso rises. Read from above, this shows
as the helmet and shoulder line pushing upward and forward with each step, with a small settle at
the top of each stride.
Climbing under load costs effort: the pace is steady but heavy, the shoulders work, and the body
leans slightly forward into the climb rather than sitting back.
Loaded kit has mass: the plate carrier presses down on the shoulders, the rear MOLLE webbing and
loose strap ends hang straight down under gravity and swing on short damped arcs with each step,
settling within a second. Nothing floats and nothing trails.
Rifles are carried at a low ready on their slings, muzzles angled downward, and move with the body
with slight inertial lag rather than staying rigidly fixed to it.
A gloved hand runs along the railing on the turns and releases again.
Turning at each landing carries real angular momentum — the body decelerates into the turn, pivots,
and accelerates out of it, never snapping to a new heading.
Perspective changes correctly as they approach: each man grows in scale, the angle onto his helmet
and shoulders opens up, and the parallax between him and the railings shifts.
Fabric has cloth delay behind each stride. Boots make full-sole contact with the treads.
No floating motion, no frictionless feet, no weightless gear, no rubbery CG movement, no synchronised
identical walk cycles across the four men — each has his own slightly different rhythm and gait.

LIGHTING
Cold institutional fluorescent, exactly as in <<<loc_stairs2>>>, and unchanged for the whole shot.
The vertical wall tube at screen-left is the brightest source in frame, blooming along its length.
Each landing carries its own tube on its underside, and these recede down the shaft as a diminishing
chain of pale green-white bars.
Light falls straight down from these sources: it catches the tops of the helmets, the shoulders and
the upper surfaces of the plate carriers as hard cold highlights, and everything below the shoulder
line falls into deep shadow. From this angle the men read mostly as dark shapes with lit tops.
As they climb they come up out of the dim lower shaft into progressively better-lit levels — each
man gets brighter and more legible across the shot as he rises toward the camera.
The stairs immediately under each tube are brightly lit; the stretches between are dim. The bottom
of the shaft stays near-black.
Palette: cold teal-green, desaturated, high contrast, deeply crushed blacks. Gentle halation on the
tubes. Fine film grain.
Exposure is fixed for the lit stairs and never changes as the men move through the frame. No flicker
in any tube. No fill light, no beauty light, no warm accent anywhere.

AUDIO — NO SPEECH, RADIO ONLY
No music. No dialogue of any kind. None of the four operatives speaks, mouths words, keys a mic or transmits. No subtitles.
The only voices are INDISTINCT RADIO TRANSMISSIONS from units elsewhere in the building, incoming only, bleeding thinly from the helmet pickups, low in the mix and never intelligible. Heavily band-limited and compressed, one short clipped transmission around 0:01 opening and closing with a squelch break, and a single squelch break with only carrier hiss around 0:04. Flattened to rhythm and cadence only — no discernible words, no recognisable language, no callsigns, no numbers. Procedural and calm, not urgent.
Boots on concrete and steel nosings: four sets, hard and overlapping but not synchronised, ringing up the shaft with a long hollow stairwell reverb that stacks and doubles, rising in the mix as the file climbs closer.
Gear: vest creak, sling shift, a plate knock on the turn, a glove sliding on the steel handrail.
Processed helmet breath from the captain, working harder with the climb.
Distant residential ambience underneath: a low structural rumble, far-off indistinct noise from other floors, no voices, no gunfire.
