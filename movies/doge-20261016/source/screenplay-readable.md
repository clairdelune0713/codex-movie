# TART & TINE  
### *From Victoria to Victoria*

An original animated short: screenplay and shot list for AI video generation.  
**Runtime:** ~17:25 (1045 seconds) · **Scenes:** 51, each 30s or less · **Shots:** 232 · **Style:** Pixar's *Up* · **Dialogue:** English

---

## 0. How to read this script

- **One scene = one generation clip, 30 seconds max.** Each scene header gives its length, its place in the film, the location, the cast (attach those reference sheets) and the music cue.  
- **Shots are the cuts inside a scene.** Each shot header gives the shot number, its timecode inside the scene and the framing, e.g. `**3.6** · 15–19s · WIDE`. For a multi-shot model, paste the shots in order as `[0–4s] ...`, `[4–8s] ...`. If your model only makes single shots, generate each one separately and cut them together; the scene still runs 30s or less.  
- **Shot descriptions are written as prompts:** setting first, then who is in frame, then the *one* main action. Add the **Global Style Suffix** (below) to every prompt.  
- **Copy the "lock" description** from the Character Bible every time a character appears. That keeps the model consistent.  
- **Captions** (place names, "Two years later") are added in the edit, not generated.  
- **Only humans speak.** Dialogue lines are for voice recording and lip-sync; `(O.S.)` = off-screen. Animal sounds (barks, whines, howls, meows, chirps, honks) are written as *SFX* lines for the sound team.

**Global Style Suffix**  
> 3D animated feature-film frame in the style of Pixar's *Up* (2009): rounded caricatured shapes, soft warm global illumination, saturated but gentle colors, painterly skies, expressive faces and eyes, shallow cinematic depth of field, 16:9.

### Rules of the world  
1. **No animal speaks human language.** The dogs, the cat, the goose and the bird communicate only with real animal sounds and body language: ears, eyebrows, tails, head tilts, sighs and sneezes (think Gromit, or Dante in *Coco*). Their mouths never lip-sync to words. **The humans do all the talking**, and they often say out loud what the dogs are feeling: Mags talks to the puppies like they're people, Mr. Leung reads Tart through the harness, and Kiki narrates Tine.  
2. **Screen direction:** Tart moves **left → right**. Tine moves **right → left**. At the end they run straight at each other and meet in the middle.  
3. **Mr. Leung is completely blind.** He wears his sunglasses in every shot. He never looks *at* anything. His head tilts toward sounds. He finds things with his hands and holds the harness handle in his **left** hand. Every line he speaks is about sound, smell, touch or memory, never about sight.  
4. **Tart's working state:** harness ON = posture straight, eyes forward, no play. Harness OFF = a regular (silly) dog.  
5. **No slow motion anywhere.** Even the emotional beats are played at normal speed.

## 1. Story at a glance

**Logline:** Two Labrador pups grow up as best friends on a ranch in Victoria, Australia, and get split up and sent to Hong Kong to train as service dogs. Serious Tart becomes a guide dog for a blind radio host. Bouncy Tine becomes a companion dog who melts the heart of a grumpy old widower. For years they keep missing each other by seconds, until one Friday evening on Victoria Harbour a single squeaky "HONK" brings them back together.

## 6. Music & sound motifs

| Motif | Where | Notes |  
|---|---|---|  
| **"Squee-Honk"** | Everywhere | Two notes, high then low. It's the toy's sound and the film's main musical theme. Play it softly whenever either dog thinks of the other, and in full orchestra at the reunion. |  
| **Ranch waltz** | Part One, reunion, epilogue | Light, bouncy *Up*-style waltz (piano, pizzicato strings, accordion). Slowed down for the goodbye. |  
| **Sports-broadcast brass** | Mr. Leung's walks | Jazzy big-band stings under his commentary. |  
| **Ukulele & glockenspiel** | Elder centre | Sunny and silly. |  
| **Tom & Jerry pizzicato** | Goose chase, Operation Grumpy Grandpa, Boss's taunts | Sneaky plucked strings, xylophone hits on the gags. |  
| **Kookaburra laugh** | Ranch | The ranch's "laugh track." It stays silent at the goodbye and laughs once more in the stinger. |  
| **Hong Kong crossing beeps** | Scenes 25–26 | Slow "tock... tock" means wait. Fast "tik-tik-tik" means walk. This is how Mr. Leung knows when to cross. |  
| **Tram "ding-ding"** | Hong Kong scenes | The tram is the "fate blocker" in the near-misses. |  
| **Honks** | Scenes 36 and 38 | Taxi, minibus, nose-blow... then the real HONK. |

## 7. Production notes for video generation

1. **One scene = one clip of 30s or less.** Most scenes stay in a single location so the background holds steady across the cuts. Inside a scene, each shot has one main action and at most three featured characters. Crowds are soft background only.  
2. **Animal performance:** no animal ever lip-syncs to words. Prompt emotions through body language: ears up or flat, eyebrows, head tilts, tail speed, play-bows, sighs, sneezes. Bark and whine timing comes from the *SFX* lines.  
3. **Chain scenes that share a set.** When two scenes in a row use the same location (e.g. 5→6, 14→15, 19→20, 25→26→27, 33→34, 43→…→48), use the last frame of one as the first-frame reference for the next.  
4. **Reuse locations.** The film has only about ten sets (see Locations). Generate a clean "empty set" still for each and use it as an image reference so backgrounds stay consistent.  
5. **New reference sheets needed** (to match the existing ones in `ref-images/`): Puppy Tart, Puppy Tine, Mags, Boss, Gertrude, Jade, the three Mahjong Grannies, and Sir Squeaks (whole, plus the two halves).  
6. **Costume states to track:**  
   - Tart wears the **harness** in 12.1–12.2, 13.3–13.4, from 15.3 through Scene 18, and in Scenes 24–27, 30.4–30.5, 35.1–35.3, 36.1–36.3 and 37.1–39.4.  
   - Tart wears his **collar only** at home (14.1–15.2, 35.4, 36.4) and from 39.4, when Mr. Leung unclips him. He's on a **brown leash** in 49.2.  
   - Tine wears the **orange bandana with the toy tucked in** from 13.3 onward, plus the **orange leash** whenever Kiki is with him.  
   - Tine has a **dried fish on one ear** from 30.2 and a **tomato on his head** from 31.3, through the end of Scene 33.  
7. **Mr. Leung's sunglasses never come off.** Never prompt him "looking at" something. Use "head tilted toward the sound," "listening," "hands feeling."  
8. **Split screens** (11.2, 11.3, 13.3, 13.4, 36.4) are easiest to make as two separate clips composited in the edit.  
9. **Memory flashes** (43.2) can reuse frames from Scenes 1–7 with a warm golden grade and film grain. No new generation needed.  
10. **Maps and title** (10.1, 10.2, 50.1, 50.2) are easiest as 2D motion graphics over a parchment texture, so Scenes 10 and 50 barely need the video model.  
11. **Captions** are added in the edit. Video models usually render text badly.  
12. **Pacing:** shots run 2–9 seconds and scenes 6–30 seconds. If a scene comes out a few seconds long or short, that's fine: the total only needs to land around 17 minutes. Trim rather than slow anything down. There is no slow motion in this film.
